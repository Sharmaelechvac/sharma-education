Residential Subpanel Rough-In

This is the indoor panel, after the TUG. A Siemens 200A meter-main (MM0202B1200) on the garage wall feeds a Siemens indoor subpanel with a 4-wire feeder — two hots, a neutral, and an equipment grounding conductor. Central Florida house, NEC 2020 as used in the Florida Building Code, 8th Edition. Rough-in sets the can, routes the cable, and leaves the conductors long. Nothing is terminated on this visit.

Safety first: work it dead. The meter-main is service equipment the utility will energize. Keep hands out of the meter socket, and do not land conductors on a bus that is hot. The foreman and the plans win over this page.
The fail that matters: in the subpanel the neutral bar stays isolated from the can. The bonding screw or strap that ships with the panel comes out. The equipment ground gets its own bar, bonded to the enclosure. Neutral and ground meet at the service disconnect — the meter-main — and not again downstream.

Inside the can — neutral isolated

NEC 250.24(A)(5)NEC 250.142(B)BONDING SCREW OUT

The meter-main is the service disconnect. That is the one place the neutral is bonded to the enclosure and to the grounding electrode system. The indoor panel is on the load side of that disconnect. On the load side, the grounded conductor is not used to ground the can, and it is not tied to the equipment ground again.

Take the bonding screw or strap out before the cover goes on, and do not set it aside “for later” in the bottom of the can where someone will thread it back in. The neutral bar stays on its insulators. Mount the ground bar to the can with the machine screws from the kit so the bar, the screws, and the enclosure are metal to metal. At trim, neutrals land only on the isolated bar. Equipment grounds, including the feeder EGC, land only on the ground bar.

This panel is in the same house as the meter-main. It does not get its own neutral-to-ground bond, and it does not get a ground rod of its own. The green or bare conductor in the feeder is what grounds the can.

Feeder size — breaker and conductor

NEC 310.12NEC 310.16NEC 250.122MATCH THE BREAKER

The feeder is four conductors: two ungrounded, one neutral, one equipment ground. The breaker that protects the feeder sets the ampacity you have to meet. A bigger cable does not fix a breaker that is too big for the wire.

200A — whole house

100A sub — breaker and conductor together

Table 310.12 is the dwelling table. It applies to the service, and to a feeder that carries the entire load of the dwelling. On the usual Sharma house the indoor panel is that feeder: the meter-main is the disconnect, and everything in the house is downstream of this cable. For that 200A feeder the ungrounded conductors and the neutral are 4/0 aluminum. The equipment ground is sized from 250.122, not from 310.12: a 200A breaker needs at least #4 aluminum or #6 copper.

2/0-2/0-2/0-4 aluminum SER is a real cable: three 2/0 aluminum conductors and a #4 aluminum equipment ground. The #4 ground is large enough for a breaker up to 200A. The 2/0 conductors are not. Table 310.12 lists 2/0 aluminum at 150A when the feeder carries the whole dwelling. Do not land that SER on a 200A breaker. A 4/0-4/0-4/0-4 aluminum SER is the cable form of the 200A dwelling feeder.

The MM0202B1200 has a factory 200A main and two 1-inch branch spaces. A two-pole feeder breaker fills both spaces. That is the 100A example: a 100A two-pole in those spaces, and a feeder sized for 100A. If that 100A feeder carries the entire dwelling, Table 310.12 allows #2 aluminum (a 2-2-2-4 aluminum SER is the common cable). If the 100A panel feeds only part of the house — a suite, a garage shop, an addition — Table 310.12 does not apply. Use the 75°C column of Table 310.16, because breaker and lug terminations are 75°C unless the listing says otherwise. That is #1 aluminum or #3 copper. #2 aluminum is 90A at 75°C, so it is not a 100A feeder outside of Table 310.12.

This two-space can has no feed-through lug kit. The 200A whole-house feeder is protected by the 200A main, and the conductors land only on lugs the can is listed for. Do not put two conductors under a lug listed for one. If the plan shows a 200A indoor panel on this meter-main, the foreman confirms the landing before the cable is cut.

Identify the conductors and leave them that way. Black and red are the hots. The white is the neutral and stays white. The equipment ground is green or bare. If the SER gives you two black legs, phase-tape one leg red at each end so the next person can tell them apart.

Where the panel goes

NEC 110.26NEC 240.24(A)NEC 240.24(D)NEC 240.24(E)
  • Not a bathroom, and not a clothes closet. Overcurrent devices stay out of dwelling bathrooms, and out of the vicinity of easily ignitable material such as a clothes closet (240.24(D) and (E)).
  • Stud bay, plumb. On a Sharma garage the exterior wall is block. Do not bore the block for this feeder or for the branch circuits. The panel sits in a wood stud bay, and the cables leave the top of the can, through the top plate, into the attic.
  • Handle height. Set the can so the center of the grip of the highest breaker handle, in its highest position, is not more than 6 ft 7 in above the floor (240.24(A)). 404.8(A) uses that same measurement for switches. The drawing shows the breaker rule.
  • Working clearance in front of the panel, kept clear for the life of the house, not used as a shelf: 30 in wide (or the width of the can, whichever is more; the 30 in does not have to be centered, and the door still has to open 90°), 36 in deep for a 120/240-volt panel with the live parts on one side only, and 6 ft 6 in high or the height of the equipment, whichever is greater (110.26).

Rough-in steps

1 · Set the can

Height first, then plumb. Screw into the studs, not into drywall. The highest handle has to land at or under 6 ft 7 in when the breakers are in. A can that leans is the first thing the inspector sees.

2 · Knockouts and the connector

Open only the knockout you are using. On this rough-in that is a top knockout for the feeder, and the top knockouts the branch cables need for the attic. A listed connector for that cable — SER connector or NM connector, sized for the cable — with the locknut run up tight inside. Close every unused opening. An open knockout is a fail.

NEC 110.12(A)NEC 408.7

3 · Route the feeder through the attic

Out of the feeder breaker in the meter-main, up, across the attic, down the stud bay, into the top of the subpanel. Straight runs, secured, not draped. Secure the cable within 12 in of the meter-main and within 12 in of the subpanel, then at intervals not over 4 ft 6 in (334.30). Type SE cable used for this interior feeder follows those same support rules (338.10). Staples snug, not crushed through the jacket.

NEC 334.30NEC 338.10

4 · Branch cables leave the top

Same path as the feeder. Homeruns go up into the attic because the garage block is not a raceway and it is not a place you bore NM through. Where a cable is run through a wood member and the edge of the hole is less than 1-1/4 in from the nearest face, or where the cable is parallel to a stud and that close to the face, cover it with a steel plate listed for the purpose, at least 1/16 in thick (300.4). The plate is there so the drywall screw does not find the wire.

NEC 300.4

5 · Leave length — do not terminate

In the can, leave 18 to 24 in of each conductor, coiled so the ends stay clear of the bars and the bus. This is rough-in. Do not skin them back to the lug and do not torque anything. Identify them: black, red, white, green or bare. Tag each branch cable with the room and the load, specific enough that the directory can be written at trim.

ROUGH-IN LENGTH

6 · Bonding check before you leave

Bonding screw or strap removed. Neutral bar isolated. Ground bar installed and bonded to the can. Feeder is four wires, not three. If you can see a factory strap still tying the neutral bar to the enclosure, the rough-in is not done.

7 · Marks the next crew will need

Every circuit gets a clear, specific name in the directory on the face or inside of the door — the purpose of the circuit, not who uses the room (408.4(A)). Write it at trim from the tags you left. Also mark the panel with where it is fed from: “Supplied from: meter-main, garage exterior.” NEC 2020 408.4(B) requires that source-of-supply mark on feeder-fed panels in buildings other than one- and two-family dwellings, and the mark has to be permanent and not handwritten. A Sharma house is a one-family dwelling, so that section does not require the mark here. Put it on anyway. The next person has to be able to find the feeder breaker without opening every cover on the job.

NEC 408.4(A)NEC 408.4(B)

Breakers — AFCI and GFCI

NEC 210.12NEC 210.8

The branch breakers go in at trim, not at rough-in. The homerun tags are what make that possible. In a dwelling, the 120-volt, 15- and 20-amp branch circuits that supply outlets or devices in kitchens, family rooms, dining rooms, living rooms, parlors, libraries, dens, bedrooms, sunrooms, recreation rooms, closets, hallways, laundry areas, and similar rooms need AFCI protection (210.12). Sharma uses a combination-type AFCI breaker at the origin of the circuit.

GFCI protection (210.8) covers the dwelling locations that section names, including bathrooms, garages, outdoors, crawl spaces, basements, kitchen counter receptacles, receptacles within 6 ft of a sink, and laundry areas. Where one circuit needs both, a dual-function breaker does both jobs. Tag the cable for the breaker it will get. A tag that only says “bedroom” is how the wrong breaker ends up in the space.

Common rough-in fails — don't be these

  • Bonding screw left in. The neutral is then bonded at the subpanel. That is the fail. Screw out, strap out.
  • Neutrals and grounds on one bar. Isolated bar for neutrals. Bonded bar for grounds. They do not share.
  • Three-wire feeder. Two hots and a neutral, and the can has no equipment ground. This panel needs the fourth conductor.
  • 2/0 SER on a 200A breaker. 2/0-2/0-2/0-4 aluminum SER is the 150A dwelling size in Table 310.12. The 200A dwelling size is 4/0 aluminum.
  • #2 aluminum on a 100A breaker that does not feed the whole house. Outside Table 310.12, #2 aluminum is not a 100A conductor at 75°C. Use #1 aluminum or #3 copper.
  • Cable bored through the garage block. It leaves the top and uses the attic.
  • One staple at the can and a long loose run. Within 12 in, then every 4 ft 6 in.
  • Conductors cut short, or landed at rough-in. Leave 18–24 in. Do not terminate.
  • No plate where the bit came out close to the face of the stud.
  • Panel in a closet or a bathroom, handle too high, or the floor in front stacked with material. Location, 6 ft 7 in, and the clearance box are part of the rough-in, not a trim problem.
